Before you land

Free for everyone. The mistakes most visitors make in Paris.

  1. Avoid unofficial taxis at the airportAt both Charles de Gaulle and Orly, drivers will come up to you in the arrivals hall offering a ride into Paris. Never accept. Head straight to the official taxi rank outside, or book a ride through an app.
  2. Kitchens close in the afternoonFrench restaurants usually have two services, one at lunch and one in the evening. Lunch ends around 2 pm, dinner rarely starts before 8 pm, and most kitchens close around 10 or 10:30 pm. Finding a proper meal outside those hours can be tricky.
  3. Ask for "une tradition"When you walk into a bakery, ask for "une tradition", the real French baguette. If you like it lightly baked, ask for "une blanche". If you like it dark and crusty, ask for "une bien cuite".
  4. Have your coffee at the barA coffee "au comptoir" costs between €1.20 and €1.80, much less than the same one on the terrace. That's where the locals stand. And don't be surprised to see people enjoying a glass of white wine or a beer at the bar, even first thing in the morning. It's a French classic.
  5. Watch out for the classic scamsPetition clipboards, the "found" gold ring, string bracelets at Sacré Cœur. Just smile and keep walking.

My insider guide

Every place in this guide is one I've been to many times myself. I'm a Parisian, and I only share what I truly know. Nothing copied from the internet.

Left Bank

Saint Germain des Prés

My perfect Paris day, from morning coffee to late night drinks.

Rue de Buci

My favorite street in Paris. It runs between Rue de Seine and Rue de l'Ancienne Comédie, just a short walk from the river. I absolutely love this neighborhood.

Bar du Marché

My morning coffee spot, always buzzing and always Parisian. Rue de Buci and Rue de Seine feel like a village of their own, with greengrocers, cheese shops and bakers at every turn. This is the Paris we love. Don't miss it.

75 Rue de Seine (Paris 6th), on the corner of Rue de Buci

Place de Furstenberg

A pretty, peaceful little square that most visitors walk straight past. Yet tucked away in one corner is the Musée Delacroix, the painter's last home and studio, with a lovely hidden garden.

My favorite night out in Paris

Paris 6th, on the Left Bank. Between the Saint Sulpice and Mabillon metro stations, the little streets around Rue des Canettes, Rue Guisarde and Rue Princesse are the place to be on Thursday, Friday and Saturday nights. They're packed with people and full of little pubs, and the buzz lasts all night. Right in the middle sits Castel, the legendary private club. It's one of a kind here: very chic, yet surrounded by bars that are lively, fun and down to earth. That contrast is part of the charm. For a more laid back night, head to Chez Georges on Rue des Canettes, or Le Pousse au Crime. Both have little cellar bars downstairs, and the party goes on until the early hours.

Le Bon Marché

The most elegant department store in Paris. It began as a small shop, and in 1852 Aristide and Marguerite Boucicaut turned it into one of the world's first modern department stores, an idea that inspired Émile Zola's novel Au Bonheur des Dames. Under its glass roof you'll find designer fashion, beauty, homeware and art installations, and right next door, La Grande Épicerie, a food hall that's a treat in itself. When you come out, cross the little square to the Hôtel Lutetia for a drink. It was built in 1910 by the owners of Le Bon Marché to welcome their wealthiest customers, and it's still stunning today.

24 Rue de Sèvres (Paris 7th)

Musée d'Orsay

The one museum I insist on.

I'm not here to send you to lots of museums, but this one is unmissable. Take your time and explore every corner. Housed in a stunning former railway station built for the 1900 World's Fair, it holds the greatest collection of Impressionist art in the world, from Monet and Renoir to Degas and Van Gogh. Absolutely breathtaking.

Mouffetard market

My personal favorite market.

Open Tuesday through Sunday. On Sundays there's a charming little guinguette, a French tradition of music and dancing in the open air. The fresh produce, meat, fish and seafood are outstanding, and the atmosphere is fantastic. The whole Mouffetard neighborhood is full of character and well worth exploring.

Right Bank

Walk the Marais

My favorite walk on the Right Bank.

Start at Bastille

Begin at Place de la Bastille and let yourself wander through the Marais. It's one of the liveliest neighborhoods in Paris, the heart of the city's gay scene and full of style, with wonderful boutiques, cafés and bars. There's always something going on.

Place des Vosges

Don't miss it. The oldest planned square in Paris, and one of the most beautiful.

Along Rue de Rivoli

Head west along Rue de Rivoli toward the Louvre.

Palais Royal

Right across from the Louvre, it's well worth stopping here for a few minutes. It was built in the 1630s for Cardinal Richelieu, and a young Louis XIV later grew up within its walls. In July 1789, a fiery speech in its garden helped spark the French Revolution. Today, its quiet arcades, elegant garden and striped columns make it one of the most peaceful spots in the heart of Paris.

The Louvre and the Tuileries

Walk past the Louvre, then take a break in the Tuileries Garden.

Angelina

Right across from the gardens. Treat yourself to the "Africain", their legendary hot chocolate: thick, rich and served with whipped cream on the side.

226 Rue de Rivoli (Paris 1st)

Horse racing in Paris

A day at the races. Horse racing is a true French passion, and here's where I go.

Vincennes, my favorite

The home of harness racing. Go for the evening meetings in season, or on weekends, when the racing is excellent and the atmosphere electric. Book a table at the racecourse restaurant, overlooking the finish line. For small groups, a visit to the stables on race day can sometimes be arranged. Just ask me.

Longchamp and Saint Cloud

Flat racing. Head to Longchamp for the major race days.

Auteuil

Steeplechase racing, at its best in season. When the rain sets in and the track turns heavy, the racing is spectacular.

PSG game day

How I spend match day at the Parc des Princes.

Before and after: Bistrot 31

My local, right by the Église d'Auteuil, just a 10 to 15 minute walk from the Parc des Princes. It has a lovely terrace for a few beers before kickoff, a bite to eat beforehand, or dinner after the final whistle.

Getting tickets

Your safest bet is the official PSG website, or the club store at the Parc des Princes. Big matches sell out weeks ahead, so plan early. If you're stuck, drop me a message a few days before the game. I have a few connections who can often get tickets.

Where to eat ?

My six favorite tables in Paris.

Bistrot 31

My absolute favorite.

I've been coming here for 20 years, and it's run by one of my closest friends, David. You'll never be disappointed, and you'll never see a tourist. It's a little off the beaten track, but it's well worth the trip. Tell David that Bill sent you and he'll look after you, I promise. Just note that the kitchen closes between lunch and dinner.

31 Avenue Théophile Gautier (Paris 16th)
Phone: +33 1 42 24 52 31

What to order at lunch ?

The B31 burger: pesto, fresh basil, tomato, shaved parmesan, chili and melted mozzarella. The beef comes from the butcher next door, lean, tender and full of flavor. Ask for a side of the homemade mashed potatoes. They're incredible.

Perfect before a game

It's about a 15 minute walk from the Parc des Princes and the Stade Jean Bouin, so it's ideal before a PSG match or a rugby game.

Café des Ministères

For food lovers.

Food critics love it, and for good reason: this is classic French cooking at its very best. A stone's throw from the National Assembly, it's a favorite with journalists and politicians. Expect generous dishes from the South West and rich sauces: the famous vol au vent with sweetbreads, an award winning stuffed cabbage, and profiteroles to finish. Roxane's wine list is superb. Closed on weekends, so book ahead. You can't go wrong.

Brasserie Lipp

A Saint Germain institution.

Right across from Les Deux Magots. Lipp opened in 1880, and its dining room, with hand painted tiles, mirrors and brass, is a listed historic monument. It's a true French brasserie straight out of the early 1900s, where writers, artists and politicians have been regulars for over a century. The meat is excellent, the wine list is outstanding and the prices are fair. Best of all, the kitchen stays open all day, from noon until late, so you can sit down for a meal at 4 pm. A must.

151 Boulevard Saint Germain (Paris 6th)

A day in Champagne

A day trip you simply can't miss when you're in Paris.

The heart of Champagne

Reims is the capital of the Champagne region, and the vineyards between Reims and the little village of Ludes are dotted with Champagne cellars, from the great houses to small family estates.

Champagne Gaidoz Forget

My favorite estate. A tour of the estate, followed by a tasting.

Ludes, near Reims

Getting there

About 45 minutes by TGV from Paris to Reims, then a short drive to Ludes. Or about 1 hour 45 minutes by car from Paris.

What to avoid

The tourist traps and dreary corners I'd steer clear of.

Not worth your time

Save your time, your money and your mood.

The Champs Élysées

The same chain stores you'll find anywhere in the world, and overpriced cafés.

Rue de la Huchette and Saint Michel

Waiters touting for business on the street and mediocre tourist menus.

Around the Eiffel Tower

Expensive restaurants that aren't worth it, around the tower and Trocadéro alike. Walk a few streets away and you'll eat far better.

Menus with photos

A menu in five languages with pictures of every dish is rarely a good sign.

Châtelet Les Halles

A huge, crowded underground shopping mall, and a hotspot for pickpockets.

The Gare du Nord area at night

Along with Gare de l'Est, Barbès and La Chapelle: grimy, hectic and nothing to see. Not necessarily dangerous, just not pleasant.

Pigalle

Along Boulevard de Clichy, it's mostly sex shops and touts. Snap your photo of the Moulin Rouge from across the street and move on.
